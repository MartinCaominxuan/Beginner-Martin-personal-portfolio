IP Alive

Extending the emotional lifecycle of physical character collectibles through persistent interaction—while asking who controls memory, authenticity, spending, and attachment.

Interactive Version 8 demo · No production AI, storage, or payments

01 · Problem

Purchase creates a moment. The relationship often has no next chapter.

Physical character collectibles can hold identity, memory, and affection, but their ongoing interaction is limited once they reach a shelf. I began exploring whether the object could become an entry point to a persistent digital character—not simply a chatbot placed beside merchandise.

Purchase → Open → Display → Forget
could become
Purchase → Activate → Interact → Develop continuity

The opportunity is also risky. A familiar character can invite trust, disclosure, spending, and emotional reliance. Those effects cannot be treated as secondary policy questions; they shape the product architecture.

02 · Exploratory research

The survey established a question, not proof of a solution.

165Total responses collected
150Responses retained as valid
1Exploratory evidence source—not validation

The survey examined how people described their connection to physical blind-box products, figures, and collectibles after purchase. Results suggested that some owners experience a decline in active connection, supporting further investigation of the problem space.

I did not treat these responses as evidence that IP Alive would solve the problem. They informed hypotheses, feature priorities, and future interview questions.

03 · Concept

A character that participates in daily life without pretending to be human.

This is the latest exported React Native/Expo prototype—not a concept illustration. Try character selection, chat, memory, match-three play, affinity progression, wardrobe, mail, shop, language, and account views.

Open full demo

Version 8 connects play with relationship progression and unlockable rewards while retaining visible memory and account controls. Production AI, persistent servers, payment ports, and commissioned art are intentionally outside the current implementation.

04 · Product system

Character authenticity is a governed system, not a prompt.

01

Physical activation

A collectible or activation card establishes access.

02

Character rules

Approved personality, tone, values, and fictional-world limits.

03

User control

Visible memory, consent, correction, deletion, and exit.

04

Ongoing value

Conversation, routines, events, travel, and play.

I reframed the concept from a single consumer blind-box app toward a potential enterprise platform for character-IP owners. That direction remains a business hypothesis—not a claim of partnership or market validation.

05 · Responsible interaction

A product designed to sustain attachment must also make leaving possible.

RiskDesign response
Emotional dependencyAvoid exclusivity language, guilt loops, and claims that the character replaces human relationships.
Opaque memoryMake remembered information visible, editable, consented, and removable.
Monetized affectionSeparate relationship progression from paid progression and disclose commercial mechanics.
Minors and spendingRequire age-aware protections, guardian controls where appropriate, and clear purchase boundaries.
Unauthorized IP useUse original placeholders publicly unless explicit rights and approval are secured.
Core principle

Availability is not reciprocity. The system may support continuity and everyday routines, but it should not represent itself as a human relationship.

06 · Status and next tests

The demo is complete enough to expose the riskiest assumptions.

Current work demonstrates product reasoning, interaction architecture, and a broad high-fidelity prototype. The next evidence should come from qualitative interviews, comprehension testing for memory and monetization controls, and longitudinal observation of whether repeated character interaction feels useful, burdensome, or manipulative.

Because I see commercial potential in the concept, selected case-study material is public while detailed implementation and partner strategy remain controlled.
