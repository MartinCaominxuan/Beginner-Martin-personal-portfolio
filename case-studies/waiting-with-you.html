Waiting With You

How might a recruitment system support candidates during unresolved waiting—without inventing certainty or turning anxiety into a hiring signal?

Runnable portfolio prototype · AI and email simulated
Role
Independent product designer and developer
Context
Internship take-home redesigned after submission
Methods
Journey mapping, workflow design, ethical analysis
Tools
Python, Streamlit, Pandas, GitHub
On this page OriginReframePrototypePrinciplesEvaluationReflection

01 · Origin

The product problem appeared after the assignment ended.

During one internship application season, I submitted applications to many companies and spent long periods without decisions. I repeatedly refreshed portals, checked email, and tried to interpret small changes in status.

During the same period, one company asked me to design an AI-assisted recruitment system as an interview assignment. My first version focused on the most visible organizational needs: collecting candidate information, structuring assessment, routing applicants, and helping recruiters review results. I did not connect that design to my own experience of waiting.

After all of my summer applications ended in rejection, I returned to the prototype. The technical flow worked, but the human boundary was wrong: I had treated the recruiter as the primary user and the candidate largely as information moving through a pipeline.

A system can function normally while silence becomes a consequential part of the user experience.

02 · Reframe

From screening efficiency to a two-sided waiting system.

Version 1

Recruiter-centered routing

  • One-time candidate classification
  • Résumé and behavioral assessment
  • Efficiency as the main success criterion
  • Candidate experience ends at submission

Version 2

Candidate and recruiter workflow

  • Five-stage application progress
  • Confirmed facts separated from estimates
  • Support throughout unresolved waiting
  • Human review for consequential decisions

The redesign did not assume that more communication could remove uncertainty. Instead, it asked what the system could responsibly clarify: what is confirmed, what is estimated, what action—if any—is available, and when silence should trigger supportive communication.

04 · Product principles

Behavior can guide support without becoming evidence against the user.

Label uncertainty

Confirmed workflow information, estimates, and demonstration-only content must remain visibly distinct.

Separate support from judgment

Refresh counts may personalize reassurance, but are never shared with decision-makers or used in evaluation.

Keep a human accountable

AI structures information; high-impact and borderline decisions require human review.

Design boundary

Repeated refreshing is interpreted only as a possible sign of unmet information needs—not as a diagnosis, personality trait, or measure of candidate quality.

06 · Reflection

My most important iteration was redefining who counted as a user.

Waiting With You taught me that empathy is not a decorative message added after the workflow is built. It changes system boundaries, data rules, status language, and the definition of success. It also taught me to state where evidence ends: the prototype demonstrates a design argument, not that its support will reduce anxiety.
